# model.examples

Thirteen complete `.pwmodel` documents under `Examples/pwmodel/` in the plugin folder each carry a header stating the decisions and render that forced them. The comments are the reference: twelve read as the object they name; one is a documented near-miss.

## Start here

- **Smallest complete files:** `chess_rook` — one turned profile, one crenellated drum, three parts — and `origami_crane`, 88 triangles of hand-written buffers.
- **The heavy boolean ones:** `pipe_junction` (17,240 triangles) and `spur_gear` (15,528). Both are "cast solid, cut afterwards", and both are worth reading for how a cutting tool is built and arrayed *inside* the boolean block rather than around it.
- **The machine-generated tier:** `mobius_band` — `procedural_mesh`, one `append_buffers` line of 400 positions, then surgery by index. Its contract is different from every other file here, and [`model.explicit-geometry`](model.explicit-geometry.md) is the page for it. Do not hand-edit its numbers; regenerate the part.

## What each one is the example of

- **`amphora`** — `sweep` plus `mirror`. One strap handle is authored on +X and mirrored; the frame convention is written out with the probe that established it (a profile's `(u, v)` lands on each frame's local Y and Z, and the sweep advances along local +X). It also shows why `self_union` has to run before the mirror: siblings in a part are appended, not unioned.
- **`chess_rook`** — a turned profile, and the corpus's clearest case of vertex colour deciding a shape decision. The crown is a `cylinder` rather than a `revolve` because a revolve arrives with one polygroup per quad, so anything that walks polygroup edges works on all 48 columns at once. Both its `bevel` ops were then deleted anyway — chamfer faces carry no vertex colour and no op can paint them, so they rendered as white rings on a boxwood crown — and both its boolean tools gained `color=`, without which the dished top and the six crenellation slots came out white as well. Also the cheapest z-fighting lesson in the corpus — two cylindrical surfaces at the same radius over a shared height render as a sawtooth ridge, and the fix is a small honest step, never coincidence.
- **`crystal_cluster`** — `array_along_path` where per-copy rotation is the point (seven druse spurs, each at its own angle off the flank), and both ends of `split_normals` in one file: `split_angle=0` on the crystals against `34` on the rock. Per-crystal `color=` shows the vertex-colour path. The `spherify` → `stretch` → `remesh_uniform` → `noise_deform` chain is the worked answer to "why did my displaced box come out lumpy in a grid pattern".
- **`driftwood`** — the deformer stack: `stretch`, `taper`, `bend` twice, `twist`, three `noise_deform` passes (two surface octaves and one `apply_along_normal=false` axis wander), and one `relax`. It no longer runs `subdivide`, `smooth` or `simplify_mesh` — `steps` on the `revolve` buys the density those were there for at a quarter of the triangles. The rule it exists to teach is **ordering**, in both directions. Every boolean runs first, against a surface whose radius is a number in the source; a tool aimed at a deformed limb raises `PWMODEL_BOOLEAN_NO_EFFECT`, which aborts the part. Then the grain noise runs *after* the cuts, and that placement is what makes the fissures read: a box swept along a straight line has planar walls, straight lips and square ends, and renders as a milled slot whatever `color=` it carries. Displacing those walls afterwards is the only thing that fixed it — one four-line block moved, 12,790 → 12,518 triangles, health unchanged.
- **`gothic_window`** — `array_along_path` again, and here the argument against `array_linear` is explicit: the four saddle bars are not evenly spaced and each carries its own pitch, which an offset-only op cannot say. The header's two boolean rules — siblings append rather than union, and shapes must overlap or clear but never touch exactly — are the two that every other file in the corpus depends on.
- **`mobius_band`** — the explicit-buffer tier and the only file that uses it. A half twist is the one construction the op vocabulary cannot express, which is what earns the exception. The twist is measurable without looking: the section stands vertical at theta 180, which is why the model's Z half-extent (54.07) is the band's 54 half-width. **Regenerated 2026-08-21 at 108 wide on the 180 mean radius, a width-to-radius ratio of 0.60, and it now reads.** The former 0.42-on-76 with a 16-thick section read as a bowl rim or a wide bangle, and the standing advice was to *narrow* the strip so its two legs separate where they cross. Measured across seven shapes at one pinned three-quarter, narrowing makes it worse at every width tried — 0.27 is a bangle whether the section is 8 thick or 3, because the only visible evidence of a half twist is the band's own surface rotating from flat to vertical, and narrowing removes the surface that carries it. What reads is a wide, thin ribbon: at 0.60 the band stands as a tall wall at theta 180 and lies as a broad sheet at theta 0, and one frame contains both. There is a ceiling too — past about 0.7 the flat half covers the hole and it reads as a scoop — so the window is roughly 0.55–0.67. The file's header carries the whole ladder. Do not hand-edit its numbers; regenerate the part.
- **`oil_lamp`** — build order as a correctness property, and the corpus's clearest case of a blocker that turned out to be an ordering bug. The nozzle is unioned into the reservoir *first*, then `shell` puts a 2.6 wall inside the result, and only then do the two holes open it; hollow the reservoir first and the nozzle has no oil channel. **The shoulder notches that make the plan a keyhole are cut between the union and the `shell`.** The file recorded for several revisions that they could not be cut at all — they would open the shelled reservoir — and that is true only *after* `shell`. Cut before it, the notch becomes part of the closed solid `shell` then walls, so the 2.6 wall follows the notched boundary; nothing else had to change. Its collar is a `pipe`, but read that block with the one below it: the `bevel` the swap was made for was deleted for rendering white, so the pipe now carries a rolled-rim argument for a rim that is sharp.
- **`origami_crane`** — hand-written `append_buffers` panels with the winding arithmetic spelled out, and whole-mesh `extrude` on an *open* sheet, where duplicate-and-wall is the correct thickening and passing a matching `face_direction` returns a degenerate result. It is also the worked example of `extrude direction` setting the shell's facing: get that sign wrong and the part is inside-out with an identical render, identical counts and a clean `health`.
- **`pipe_junction`** — the boolean-heavy end. Every limb is cast solid and bored afterwards, for the same reason a foundry does it that way; the crotch is the `intersection` of an oversize run and an oversize branch; and its seven hand-written `hull` blocks are the counter-example to `ships_wheel`'s `auto`.
- **`ships_wheel`** — `self_union` used twice inside one boolean block: five overlapping turned sections resolve into one spoke, the spoke is arrayed eight times, and the eight resolve again before the result becomes a boolean operand. It is also the file that measures `split_normals split_angle=` against the angles the part actually contains, with a 5-degree margin that has already been lost once.
- **`spiral_stair`** — `extrude_along_spline` on a helix. Every frame carries `pitch=22.43` — `atan(rise / arc length)` — because a frame left at pitch 0 sections the rail vertically and the tube comes out sheared. `array_linear` climbs the collars; `set_vertex_color set_all` is the only way to colour geometry a non-generator op produced.
- **`spur_gear`** — a rack cutter subtracted 24 times, where the tooth space is the `intersection` of two half-space boxes because that is its definition. `weld_vertices` before `bevel` is the detail worth stealing: without it the chamfer stops dead at every boolean seam and you get 24 fragments instead of one continuous edge.
- **`watchtower`** — measured primitive placement. `stairs` runs along +X with its width along Y, the transpose of what the parameter doc reads like, and finding that out is what removed a `ramp` from the file. Also `pipe` for the parapet ring, and `cone top_radius=4` rather than 0, which is where the model's entire former degenerate count came from.

## What still falls short, and what was upgraded

Verdicts move only against renders described in the file that moves them. A documented near-miss teaches more than a claimed success; an un-upgraded pass teaches nothing.

- **`driftwood` stays marginal, and the blocker moved.** It is no longer that the checks are too small to see — moving them onto the broad faces fixed that, and the reason they still did not read is recorded above: a straight prism cuts planar walls, straight lips and square ends, so five correctly proportioned splits rendered as milled slots. Running the grain noise after the cuts fixed *that*. What is left is **the break faces on the two small limbs**. An oblique plane through a round limb is a flat, clean-rimmed ellipse, and at an 8-unit radius there is no room for a gouge to break it — every tool big enough to bite is bigger than the section and bores the stub hollow. Rendered close, `stub_b` is a length of cut plastic pipe and the `knob` is a plug pushed into the trunk. The two long limbs do not have the problem; their breaks are wide enough to carry gouges. The one untried lever is raising those two limbs' noise magnitudes, which trades against the pebbling and shark-fin failures the header records, so it wants its own render pass.
- **`oil_lamp` was upgraded to reads, and the upgrade is the notch.** The near-miss recorded here was that it could be called a gravy boat on silhouette alone, because the keyhole plan a Roman lamp wants needs shoulder notches and those would open the shelled reservoir. They do not — see its bullet above; that was an ordering bug, not a geometric limit. With the keyhole cut, five whole-object views at pinned exposure read as a Roman oil lamp: a gravy boat is open on top and this is closed, with a collared filling hole in a sunken discus and a long blunt nozzle. **The residual is cosmetic and named in the file: the notch face reads as a hard-edged facet from a true side elevation** — subtracting a convex tool from a convex body always leaves a crease, this one is well over the 40 degrees `split_normals` hardens at, and the three ways out (a shallower cut, a tool slid to the junction, two overlapping subtracts) are each worked through and each fails. It reads as a scallop from anything above the horizon, which is why it ships.

## Materials: all thirteen render in colour

**Every one of the thirteen now binds a vertex-colour material and renders in colour.** The corpus used to point every slot at engine content — `/Engine/BasicShapes/BasicShapeMaterial`, `/Engine/EngineMaterials/WorldGridMaterial`, `/Engine/EngineMaterials/M_Grid` — which compiled anywhere and rendered grey, dark or gridded. That is no longer true and the older description of it is wrong.

The bindings are the six instances under `/Game/PinWrightExamples/Materials/`, over two masters that put Vertex Color into Base Color: `MatteDielectric`, `SatinDielectric`, `Cloth`, `PolishedMetal`, `RoughMetal`, `Glass`. Slot **names** are still the contract and the paths are still not.

**Two mechanisms, and the division is the whole of it.** A **slot** is a shading *behaviour* — metal against cloth against glass, different roughness and metallic. **`color=` on an op is a tint within one slot.** Getting this backwards is what the old corpus did: it spent three *different* engine materials on `watchtower`'s stone, slate and timber purely to keep them from shading alike, when all three are matte dielectrics that wanted one behaviour and three colours. `watchtower` and `driftwood` now bind every slot to one instance and carry the difference in `color=`; `spiral_stair` collapses two of three that way. `gothic_window`, `chess_rook`, `oil_lamp`, `pipe_junction`, `ships_wheel` and `spur_gear` keep genuinely distinct behaviours, because no tint turns a dielectric into a metal or an opaque surface into a translucent one.

Both files that shipped no `materials { }` block now have one, and neither lost its lesson:

- **`origami_crane`** binds a single `Paper` slot. The old argument — "a one-slot model gains nothing from naming its one slot" — held only while the alternative was engine content: untagged geometry lands in a synthetic `Default` slot bound to `WorldGridMaterial`, and that slot cannot be reached by name from a file that never mentions it, so the price of not naming the slot was a paper crane rendering as grey grid.
- **`mobius_band`** binds the synthetic slot itself — `materials { Default = … }` — and tags nothing. That keeps the file's measured lesson exactly: `append_triangle` accepts neither `material=` nor `color=`, so tagging the swept body splits the twelve aperture-wall triangles into a second slot and the bore renders in a different material. Tag nothing, bind `Default`, and the whole model is one slot (`materialSlots: 1`, verified).

**What is still invisible, and what changed.** The instances carry no textures at all, which removes the trap `driftwood` spent four versions inside — a stock grid texture mistaken for a mesh defect — rather than working around it. `M_Grid` and `WorldGridMaterial` were world-space and never sampled a UV channel, so no `uv scale=` could be seen through them; nothing in the corpus binds them now, but a UV scale still shows nothing through an untextured instance, so `spiral_stair`'s and `watchtower`'s byte-identical UV measurements stand for a new reason.

**`color=` reaches only geometry a GENERATOR made, and that is a live limitation with visible consequences.** A tool inside a `union { }` does not inherit the tint of the op it is unioned onto, so every generator in the corpus carries its own `color=`, including the ones inside boolean blocks. Worse, `set_vertex_color set_all=true` does not reliably close the gap: measured, it does not reach `extrude_along_spline` output, `bevel` chamfers or `shell` inner walls, and no diagnostic reports it. Two examples record the consequence in place — `spiral_stair`'s swept handrail cannot be tinted at all and is left the default white, and `oil_lamp` dropped a collar bevel that was rendering as a white ring on a terracotta lamp. [`model.vertex-color`](model.vertex-color.md) covers the slot-versus-tint division in full.

**Metals need an environment.** `PolishedMetal` and `RoughMetal` are `Metallic: 1` and therefore render whatever is around them: with no sky light and no reflection capture they come back near-black, which reads as a broken binding and is not one. `pipe_junction`, `ships_wheel`, `spur_gear` and two thirds of `spiral_stair` are affected. Judge them in a lit rig — see [`visual-review.model-rig`](visual-review.model-rig.md) — and do not "fix" a metal that is merely unlit.

## Op coverage

The corpus exercises **57 of the 74 entries in the op table, 77%** — 52 of 68 part-context ops and 5 of 6 collision elements. Sixteen part-context ops are deliberately unexercised and every one has a measured reason. Both tables, and the reasons, are on [`model.examples.op-coverage`](model.examples.op-coverage.md).

## Compiling one

```
model.validate  { filePath: "Plugins/PinWright/Examples/pwmodel/chess_rook.pwmodel" }
model.compile   { filePath: "...", outputPath: "/Game/YourFolder/SM_ChessRook" }
```

`model.validate` runs the real engine ops on real meshes and only skips asset creation, so it is the right surface for iterating and it is where the triangle counts above came from. Read the warnings rather than the success flag. Measured 2026-08-21 across all thirteen, only five raise anything at all: `crystal_cluster` 11 × `PWMODEL_UNUNIONED_OVERLAP` plus a collision-budget note, `ships_wheel` 3 × the same plus its own budget note, `origami_crane` 2 × the whole-mesh `extrude` note, `spiral_stair` the `complex_as_simple` note, and `driftwood` 5 × `PWMODEL_STAGE_WARNING` — one per part, `'taper': Freed 2 unparented normal element(s)`, which is the crash fix reporting itself rather than a defect. `oil_lamp` raises none. Every one of them is correct about what it measures and wrong about the file — interpenetrating crystals and a boss on a cone are the design, `auto` is a per-piece ceiling, an open sheet is what whole-mesh `extrude` is for, and a stair is not a rigid body. `mobius_band` raises **none**; an older note here claimed nine `PWMODEL_UNUNIONED_OVERLAP` on it, and that has not been true since it moved to `procedural_mesh`.

## Comments that have gone stale

The headers are dated evidence. The stale claims below were corrected in the files; the last two remain here so nobody reintroduces them.

- **A header can contradict itself two paragraphs apart, and both halves get quoted.** Audited 2026-08-21 on `oil_lamp` and `driftwood`, which had four such pairs between them. `oil_lamp`'s collar block argued for `pipe` over `cylinder` *because* one `bevel` would then roll the bore rim — and the block immediately below it records that same bevel being deleted for rendering white. Its `split_normals` comment put the discus rim at 45.4 degrees where the profile block eight lines up had 59.2 (the profile block was right; the real turns over 40 are 44.4, 59.2, 82.4 and 98.8, so the margin below the threshold is 2.9 degrees, not the 8 the wrong number implied). `driftwood` said the preview scene has "next to no fill" in one bullet and measured the cold-capture artefact that causes that misreading three bullets later. The lesson is procedural: **when a paragraph is corrected, grep the rest of the file for the claim it just replaced.** Every one of these was introduced by an edit that fixed one site.
- **A figure quoted inside a historical argument still reads as current.** `oil_lamp` had `signedVolume +28904` (now `+30,372`) and `6,560` triangles "for the whole model" (now 8,244) sitting in paragraphs about past decisions. Each now says which measurement it is and when, and a figure that is a delta rather than a total is labelled as one.
- **"It reads at whole-object framing" is not a verdict.** `driftwood`'s rule 5 treated visibility as the finish line and shipped five splits that were unmissable *and* read as machining. Say what a feature reads **as**, not that it reads. The two white-geometry causes `oil_lamp` records are the same shape of problem in reverse: neither appears in any compile response, and a close render down through the collar is the only thing that confirms both are fixed.

- **`mirror` reverses the copy's winding — corrected.** It once did not, and `gothic_window`, `pipe_junction` and `spur_gear` all carried paragraphs saying a mirrored half is inside-out and acts as an anti-solid inside a boolean tool. All three now record the fix instead. `pipe_junction` took the op back — its two `array_radial count=2` workarounds are real `mirror` lines, measuring 6 triangles *cheaper* at 0 degenerates. `spur_gear` deliberately kept its rotation: same file, one line apart, `array_radial count=2 axis=x` gives 15,528 triangles / 0 degenerate against `mirror axis=z weld=false` at 15,560 / 8, because the pocket tool's face sits exactly on the blank's at z = ±30 and the two vertex orderings tessellate that coincidence differently. **Measure per model; neither op is generally better.** Note also that `PWMODEL_UNUNIONED_OVERLAP` can never fire on `mirror` or any `array_*` — all four are modifiers and the check runs only from the generator path — so `health.boundaryEdges` and a render are the only signals that an append buried a face.
- **The boolean ops publish `simplify_output`, defaulting on — corrected.** `spiral_stair`'s handrail comment said PinWright pins the engine's boolean to `bSimplifyOutput=false` and gives `.pwmodel` no way to turn it back on. All four boolean ops now carry the parameter and it defaults **true**, the engine's own default. The 135,324-against-2,608 figure it quoted was not wrong, but it measured the *pinned-off* behaviour and no longer describes the default. Re-measured on that exact geometry: append 3,072, union at the default 7,740, union with `simplify_output=false` 135,324. The append still wins — but on a 2.4x margin for deleting sixteen walls buried inside an opaque column, not a fifty-fold one.
- **`pipe` is a closed, centred solid.** Several files carry a "this used to be broken" paragraph and are correct — the rehabilitation is already written up in `oil_lamp`, `pipe_junction`, `spur_gear` and `watchtower`. Do not repeat the old claim that `pipe` returns uncapped walls placed by their base.
- **`inset` tears a closed solid open.** `watchtower` records 272 boundary edges from `inset` alone on its parapet, reported as a clean compile. That is why it is on the unexercised list rather than in the file.
